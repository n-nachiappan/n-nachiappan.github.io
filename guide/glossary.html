Reference

Glossary & keyword index

Key terms used throughout the guide, each linked to the chapter that covers it in depth.

# A B C D E F G H I L M P Q R S T U V W

#

10-year rule
The SECURE Act requirement that most non-spouse heirs empty an inherited IRA within 10 years.
→ Chapter 26. Estate Taxes and How Each Account Is Treated
4% rule
A rule of thumb for a sustainable initial withdrawal rate; Bengen's updated 'Universal SAFEMAX' is 4.7%.
→ Chapter 7. The 4% Rule, and How to Actually Execute It
401(k) / 403(b) / 457(b) / TSP
Employer-sponsored, tax-advantaged retirement plans funded by payroll deferrals; the 403(b), 457(b), and federal TSP are close cousins of the 401(k).
→ Chapter 1. Employer Plans: The 401(k) and Its Cousins

A

ACA premium tax credit (PTC)
An income-based subsidy that lowers the monthly premium for a health plan bought on the ACA marketplace — the key tool for affordable pre-Medicare coverage in early retirement.
→ Chapter 22. Health Insurance Before Medicare: The ACA Bridge
Annual gift exclusion
The amount ($19,000/recipient in 2026) you can gift yearly without touching your lifetime exemption.
→ Chapter 26. Estate Taxes and How Each Account Is Treated
Annuity
An insurance contract that converts a lump sum into guaranteed income for life; a SPIA starts immediately, a QLAC starts later and defers RMDs.
→ Chapter 15. Annuities: Buying Your Own Guaranteed Income
Asset location
Placing tax-inefficient assets in sheltered accounts and tax-efficient ones in taxable accounts.
→ Chapter 17. Withdrawal Sequencing Across Account Types

B

Backdoor Roth
Funding a Roth despite income limits by contributing to a non-deductible IRA and converting it.
→ Chapter 3. Roth Accounts and the Backdoor

C

Capital gains
Profit on an investment, taxed only when sold; long-term gains (held 1+ year) get preferential 0/15/20% rates.
→ Chapter 6. Personal Savings: Taxable Accounts, Treasuries, and Inflation Bonds
COBRA
The right to keep your former employer's health plan for up to 18 months after leaving — comprehensive but usually expensive, since you pay the full unsubsidized premium.
→ Chapter 22. Health Insurance Before Medicare: The ACA Bridge
COLA
The annual Social Security cost-of-living adjustment (2.8% for 2026), tied to CPI-W.
→ Chapter 5. Social Security Basics: How Benefits Are Earned (and the Spousal 50%)
Conversion ladder
Spreading Roth conversions across several low-tax years to smooth the rate and stay under thresholds.
→ Chapter 12. Roth Conversions
CPI-U / CPI-W / R-CPI-E
Inflation indices for all urban consumers, urban wage earners (used for COLA), and the elderly, respectively.
→ Chapter 8. Inflation: Why Your Number Is Probably Wrong

D

Defined-benefit (DB) pension
An employer plan that pays a formula-based income for life; the employer bears the investment and longevity risk.
→ Chapter 14. Pensions: Guaranteed Income You Can't Outlive
Delayed retirement credits
The ~8%/year benefit increase for claiming Social Security after FRA, up to age 70.
→ Chapter 13. When to Claim Social Security
Domicile
Your legal permanent home — the state that gets to tax your retirement income. Changing it (not merely owning a second place) is what escapes a former state’s tax.
→ Chapter 19. State Taxes on Retirement Income

E

Employer match
Money your employer adds to your 401(k) based on your own contributions — an immediate, guaranteed return you should always capture in full.
→ Chapter 1. Employer Plans: The 401(k) and Its Cousins
Equity risk premium
The extra long-run return stocks deliver over bonds for bearing their volatility.
→ Chapter 24. The Power of Compounding and Historical Returns
Estate tax exemption
The amount ($15M/person in 2026) you can transfer free of federal estate tax.
→ Chapter 26. Estate Taxes and How Each Account Is Treated
Exclusion ratio
The formula that makes part of each non-qualified annuity payment a tax-free return of principal and part taxable earnings.
→ Chapter 15. Annuities: Buying Your Own Guaranteed Income

F

Form 8606
The IRS form that tracks non-deductible (after-tax) IRA basis so it isn't taxed again later.
→ Chapter 2. IRAs: Traditional and Spousal
Form SSA-44
The appeal to have Medicare use current (lower) income after a life-changing event.
→ Chapter 11. IRMAA: The Medicare Surcharge Cliff
Full Retirement Age (FRA)
The age (67 for those born 1960+) at which you receive your full, unreduced benefit.
→ Chapter 5. Social Security Basics: How Benefits Are Earned (and the Spousal 50%)

G

Guardrails
Spending rules (e.g., Guyton-Klinger) that adjust withdrawals up or down to protect the portfolio.
→ Chapter 23. Sequence-of-Returns Risk and How to Blunt It

H

HDHP
High-Deductible Health Plan — required coverage to contribute to an HSA.
→ Chapter 4. The HSA: Your Stealth Retirement Account
Healthcare inflation
The ~5–6% annual rise in medical costs that outpaces general inflation over a retirement.
→ Chapter 9. Healthcare Costs and Inflation
HECM
Home Equity Conversion Mortgage — the FHA-insured reverse mortgage for homeowners 62+; non-recourse, with a lump-sum, line-of-credit, term, or lifetime tenure payout.
→ Chapter 16. Home Equity as Retirement Income
HSA
Health Savings Account — the only triple-tax-advantaged account: pre-tax in, tax-free growth, tax-free medical withdrawals (federally; California and New Jersey don't conform and tax contributions and growth).
→ Chapter 4. The HSA: Your Stealth Retirement Account

I

I bonds
Inflation-indexed U.S. savings bonds ($10,000/person/year) whose rate resets with inflation every six months.
→ Chapter 6. Personal Savings: Taxable Accounts, Treasuries, and Inflation Bonds
IRD (Income in respect of a decedent)
Inherited pre-tax account dollars that are taxed as ordinary income to the heir, with no step-up.
→ Chapter 26. Estate Taxes and How Each Account Is Treated
IRMAA
Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount — an income-based surcharge on Medicare Part B and D premiums.
→ Chapter 11. IRMAA: The Medicare Surcharge Cliff

L

Longevity risk
The risk of outliving your money — the master risk that magnifies inflation, healthcare, and sequence risk.
→ Chapter 25. The Long Tail: Longevity Risk and the Retiree Inflation Gap

M

MAGI
Modified Adjusted Gross Income — AGI plus tax-exempt interest; the figure that drives IRMAA and many phase-outs.
→ Chapter 11. IRMAA: The Medicare Surcharge Cliff
Mega backdoor Roth
Using after-tax 401(k) contributions (up to the $72,000 combined limit) converted to Roth, if the plan allows.
→ Chapter 3. Roth Accounts and the Backdoor

P

PBGC
Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation — the federal insurer that backstops private defined-benefit pensions up to an annual cap (~$93,477 at 65 in 2026).
→ Chapter 14. Pensions: Guaranteed Income You Can't Outlive
PIA (Primary Insurance Amount)
Your Social Security benefit at full retirement age, computed from your 35 highest earning years.
→ Chapter 5. Social Security Basics: How Benefits Are Earned (and the Spousal 50%)
Portability
A surviving spouse's ability to use a deceased spouse's unused estate-tax exemption.
→ Chapter 26. Estate Taxes and How Each Account Is Treated
Premium tax credit — see ACA premium tax credit
The ACA subsidy that caps a marketplace plan's cost as a percentage of your income; larger at lower MAGI, which makes managing income in early retirement especially valuable.
→ Chapter 22. Health Insurance Before Medicare: The ACA Bridge
Pro-rata rule
The rule that taxes a conversion across all your pre-tax and after-tax IRA money proportionally — the main backdoor-Roth trap.
→ Chapter 3. Roth Accounts and the Backdoor

Q

QCD
Qualified Charitable Distribution — a tax-free gift from an IRA (70½+) that counts toward your RMD.
→ Chapter 18. RMDs and SECURE 2.0
QLAC
Qualified Longevity Annuity Contract — a deferred annuity that hedges a long life and lowers RMDs.
→ Chapter 15. Annuities: Buying Your Own Guaranteed Income

R

Reverse mortgage
A loan that converts home equity into cash for owners 62+ with no monthly payments; the balance grows and is repaid when you sell, move out, or die.
→ Chapter 16. Home Equity as Retirement Income
RMD
Required Minimum Distribution — the mandatory taxable withdrawal from pre-tax accounts, starting at age 73.
→ Chapter 18. RMDs and SECURE 2.0
Roth 401(k)
The Roth option inside a workplace plan — no income limit, and (since 2024) no required distributions.
→ Chapter 3. Roth Accounts and the Backdoor
Roth conversion
Moving pre-tax IRA/401(k) money to Roth and paying tax now for tax-free growth later.
→ Chapter 12. Roth Conversions
Roth IRA
An account funded with after-tax dollars that grows and is withdrawn tax-free, with no lifetime required distributions.
→ Chapter 3. Roth Accounts and the Backdoor
Rule of 55
Penalty-free access to your current employer's 401(k) if you leave the job at age 55 or later.
→ Chapter 21. The Rule of 55
Rule of 72(t) / SEPP
Substantially Equal Periodic Payments — penalty-free retirement-account withdrawals before 59½.
→ Chapter 20. Rule of 72(t): Early Penalty-Free Access

S

SAFEMAX
The highest historical withdrawal rate that survived the worst retirement sequence in the data.
→ Chapter 7. The 4% Rule, and How to Actually Execute It
Senior bonus deduction
A temporary $6,000-per-person (65+) OBBBA deduction for tax years 2025–2028.
→ Chapter 10. Tax Optimization in Retirement
Sequence-of-returns risk
The danger that poor returns early in retirement do permanent damage while you're withdrawing.
→ Chapter 23. Sequence-of-Returns Risk and How to Blunt It
Social Security credit
Units earned by working (up to four per year); 40 credits (~10 years) qualify you for retirement benefits.
→ Chapter 5. Social Security Basics: How Benefits Are Earned (and the Spousal 50%)
SPIA
Single-Premium Immediate Annuity — pay a lump sum, receive guaranteed lifetime income starting right away.
→ Chapter 15. Annuities: Buying Your Own Guaranteed Income
Spousal benefit
A benefit of up to 50% of the higher earner's PIA, which tops up a lower-earning spouse.
→ Chapter 5. Social Security Basics: How Benefits Are Earned (and the Spousal 50%)
Spousal IRA
A rule letting a low- or non-earning spouse fund a full IRA using the working spouse's income, when filing jointly.
→ Chapter 2. IRAs: Traditional and Spousal
Step-up in basis
The reset of an asset's cost basis to date-of-death value, erasing a lifetime of capital-gains tax for heirs.
→ Chapter 6. Personal Savings: Taxable Accounts, Treasuries, and Inflation Bonds
Super catch-up
A larger 401(k) catch-up ($11,250 in 2026) for savers ages 60–63 under SECURE 2.0.
→ Chapter 1. Employer Plans: The 401(k) and Its Cousins
Survivor benefit
What a surviving spouse keeps — up to 100% of the deceased's benefit, including delayed-retirement credits.
→ Chapter 5. Social Security Basics: How Benefits Are Earned (and the Spousal 50%)

T

Tax torpedo
The spike in effective tax rate caused when extra income makes more of your Social Security taxable.
→ Chapter 10. Tax Optimization in Retirement
Tenure payment
A reverse-mortgage option paying a fixed monthly amount for as long as you live in the home — the annuity-like way to draw home equity.
→ Chapter 16. Home Equity as Retirement Income
TIPS
Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities — marketable Treasuries whose principal rises with the CPI.
→ Chapter 6. Personal Savings: Taxable Accounts, Treasuries, and Inflation Bonds
Traditional IRA
An individual retirement account you open yourself; contributions may be tax-deductible depending on income and workplace-plan coverage.
→ Chapter 2. IRAs: Traditional and Spousal
Treasury securities
U.S. government bills, notes, and bonds; interest is exempt from state and local tax.
→ Chapter 6. Personal Savings: Taxable Accounts, Treasuries, and Inflation Bonds

U

Uniform Lifetime Table
The IRS table of factors used to compute each year's RMD.
→ Chapter 18. RMDs and SECURE 2.0

V

Vesting
The schedule over which employer contributions become fully yours; leaving before you vest can forfeit that money.
→ Chapter 1. Employer Plans: The 401(k) and Its Cousins

W

Withdrawal sequencing
The order you draw from taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth accounts to minimize lifetime tax.
→ Chapter 17. Withdrawal Sequencing Across Account Types
