Reference · Appendix F

Using the Calculator

The idea

  • The drawdown calculator works backwards from the way real retirement feels: you name a target income you want to spend each year, and it projects whether your accounts, Social Security, and other income can fund it — net of tax, Medicare, and medical costs — all the way to your planning age.
  • It's a seven-step wizard (about two minutes) followed by a three-part results view: a plain-language verdict, the full year-by-year projection, and a save/download step.
  • This appendix is the field manual: what every input asks for, what every output number means, and — in the last table — which chapter explains each part in depth.
  • Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded or saved. Use Export scenario to keep a copy. For the math under the hood, see How the model works.

Open the tool and you'll answer seven short questions, one screen at a time, then land on your results. You can revisit any answer at any point with the ← Edit my answers button, and results recompute instantly. Read this alongside the calculator — every bold label below is a control you'll see on screen.

Part 1 · The seven inputs

The wizard steps are numbered across the top. Here's what each one asks and what to type.

StepField — what it meansChapters
1 · IncomeTarget annual income — the amount you want to spend each year in retirement, in today's dollars, after federal & state tax, Medicare, and health costs. This is the number the whole model tries to deliver. A common rule of thumb is 70–85% of what you spend now.7, 9
2 · Retire ageRetirement age — when you stop working and start drawing. If it's your current age, the model checks whether you can retire now; if later, it computes how much more to save. This age also triggers the early-access rules: 55–59½ unlocks the Rule of 55, under 55 switches to a 72(t)/SEPP schedule.23, 24, 25
3 · About youYour age now sets your birth year, RMD start, and Social Security timing. Spouse age now — fill it in to switch the whole model to married filing jointly (taxes, Medicare, and Social Security all adjust); leave blank if single.5, 14
4 · SavingsYour balances today, tracked in five buckets because each is taxed differently on the way out: Pre-tax retirement (401k / Traditional IRA — ordinary income, RMDs apply), Roth retirement (tax-free, no RMDs), Personal savings — principal (already taxed, spendable tax-free), Personal savings — unrealized capital gains (taxed at long-term rates as you sell), and HSA (pays medical costs tax-free). Leave anything you don't have blank.1–4, 6
5 · Other incomeOther taxable income (per year) — pensions, annuity payouts, rental or part-time income, dividends; anything beyond Social Security and your accounts. …of which qualified dividends / LTCG — the slice taxed at preferential rates (the rest is ordinary income). The model spends this income before touching your portfolio.15, 16, 17
6 · Social SecurityCurrent / final salary and Career start age seed an earnings record that the model runs through the real benefit formula. Est. benefit at full retirement age lets you override the computed figure with the number from your SSA statement. Expand Enter my actual year-by-year earnings to type real wages for precision. Married? Each spouse gets their own record.5, 14
7 · Assumptions & taxesThe preset (Conservative / Average / Lenient) sets return, inflation, medical trends, volatility, and longevity together — a one-click starting point. Then set your State income tax, and fine-tune Expected annual return, General inflation, Real wage growth, Plan to age (how long the money must last), and Return volatility (which drives the stress test).9, 10, 21, 8–22

The one number that matters most

Target annual income (Step 1) is spendable, after-tax, in today's dollars. Everything the model does is in service of it:

it grows your target by inflation each year, then funds it from a waterfall — other income & Social Security first, then a retirement-account draw sized to hit the target, then savings
it layers on federal & state tax, Medicare/IRMAA, and out-of-pocket medical — the draw is solved so what's left equals your target

So if you enter $90,000, the model finds whatever gross withdrawal nets you $90,000 to spend — which is why the Ret. draw in the tables is always larger than your target. Full mechanics in How the model works.

Part 2 · Reading your results

Results open on the Summary screen — the verdict — with two more screens behind it (Full details and Save / download). Start with the verdict, then dig into the tabs.

The verdict banner

A colored banner grades your plan — green (on track), yellow (close — within ~10%), or red (short) — and states the one-line reason. Below it sit the numbers that back the grade:

Tile / calloutWhat it tells you
Sustainable income / yrThe most you could safely spend every year and still last to your planning age. Compare it to your target.
% of your targetThat sustainable income as a share of what you asked for. 100%+ is green.
Savings depletedThe age your money would run out — or "never." This is the headline you don't want to see early.
Extra saving needed / yr (future retirement)If you're retiring later, the model shows this plus Nest egg needed at [age] and a savings-plan table that fills your 401(k)/IRA to the limit first.
Volatility stress testRe-runs the plan 200 times with bumpy returns and reports a success rate, decile ending balances, and the typical depletion age when it fails. The banner is pulled down to the worse of the steady-return grade and this one.
How to passWhen the stress test falls short, this box reverse-engineers the smallest fix: a cash reserve, a spending trim, working longer, or a balanced blend of all three.
Claiming / Tax / Estate calloutsOne-line summaries of the auto-optimized Social Security claiming ages, the tax-minimizing strategy (with its dollar advantage), and any estate-tax exposure — each expandable for detail.

The three detail tabs

On the Full details screen, three tabs hold the full projection. Each opens with summary tiles, then a year-by-year table (downloadable as CSV).

TabKey tiles & columns — and how to read themChapters
Accounts over timeTiles: Portfolio at retirement, Yr-1 retirement draw, Yr-1 savings draw, Balance at age [N] (what's left at the end — green if positive, red if depleted). The table tracks every account's balance and each year's draws. Watch the shading: amber rows are Rule-of-55 years, red rows are 72(t) SEPP years, and ‡ marks a forced RMD. An estate box at the bottom shows what heirs receive.20, 23, 24, 26
Social Security summaryTiles: Your PIA (benefit at full retirement age), Your benefit @ [claim age], Spouse benefit @ [age], Expected lifetime SS (survival-weighted, today's dollars). The claiming-age comparison matrix scores every claim-age combination and highlights the optimal cell — your current pick is outlined, so you can see what a different choice costs.5, 14
Income & tax ledgerTiles: Lifetime federal+state tax, Lifetime Medicare + out-of-pocket, Lifetime pre-65 premiums, Lifetime SS collected. The table is your yearly paycheck: SS, Ret. draw, Sav. draw, Roth conv., Taxable SS, Fed tax, State tax, Medicare (hover a cell for its IRMAA tier), OOP medical, Pre-65 health, and Spendable (which should match your target).11, 12, 21

Part 3 · Advanced — override the optimized strategy

By default the model auto-optimizes your Social Security claiming ages, tax strategy, and withdrawal order. On the Full details screen, expand Advanced to review or override any of it. Four panels:

PanelWhat you can changeChapters
Withdrawal strategySwitch from Target income (solve draws to hit your goal) to Plain rule, then set a fixed Plan withdrawal rate — the classic "4% rule" mode.7, 19
Social Security claiming agesOverride Claiming age — you / spouse (62–70), tweak the Real discount rate, or hit Use optimal ages to restore the recommendation.14
Tax strategyTax-bracket & IRMAA smoothing (Off / On) caps pre-tax draws under the cliffs; Roth conversions — fill the bracket (Off / 12% / 22% / 24%) converts in low-income years. Use optimal tax strategy restores the lifetime-tax-minimizing combo.11, 12, 13, 20
Health carePre-65 annual household premium, Pre-65 out-of-pocket, Out-of-pocket medical (65+), Healthcare cost inflation, and Medicare premium inflation — the levers behind every medical number in the plan.4, 10, 25

How to run a scenario comparison

The tool's real power is comparison. The pattern is always the same: run your base plan, change one input, and watch a specific number move. Try:

Timing — change Retirement age (Step 2) and watch the savings depleted age and stress success rate. Taxes — flip Advanced › Tax strategy between Off and the optimized combo and compare the Lifetime federal+state tax tile. Risk — raise Return volatility (Step 7) and read the stress test. Legacy — ladder Roth conversions and watch the Pre-tax bal column and Net to heirs shrink the future tax bill.

Use Export scenario on the last screen to save each run, then re-import to compare side by side.
