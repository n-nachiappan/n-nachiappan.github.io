Retirement

A field guide to building, spending, and passing on your savings

Retirement is really three problems, not one. First you build the savings. Then — the hard, under-taught part — you turn that pile into a paycheck that lasts the rest of your life. And finally you pass on what's left. This guide walks all three, and pairs with the calculator so you can stop reading about a decision and actually model it against your own numbers.

PART IV

Risk and the Long Run

Endurance

A retirement can run thirty years or more, and its biggest threats aren't line items you can budget for — they're the shape of your returns, the market's long-run behavior, and simply how long you live. These chapters cover the risks that compound over decades, and the buffers, evidence, and hedges that keep a very long retirement intact.

PART V

Inheritance

Legacy

Whatever you don't spend passes to others — and two separate questions decide how well. First, how it's taxed: with the federal estate tax now irrelevant for nearly every family, the real game is income-tax character and the step-up in basis, so leave the step-up and tax-free assets to your heirs and spend down or convert the pre-tax accounts yourself. Second, how it's transferred: beneficiary designations, payable- and transfer-on-death registrations, deeds, and a revocable living trust decide whether it reaches your heirs privately and in weeks — or crawls through public, months-long probate.
