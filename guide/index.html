Retirement

A field guide to building, spending, and passing on your savings

Retirement is really three problems, not one. First you build the savings. Then — the hard, under-taught part — you turn that pile into a paycheck that lasts the rest of your life. And finally you pass on what's left. This guide walks all three, and pairs with the calculator so you can stop reading about a decision and actually model it against your own numbers.

PART I

Retirement Planning

Accumulation

The years before retirement are about two things: building the pot, and building it in the right accounts. Each pillar here has its own tax treatment, contribution limits, and rules — and using them in the right order (capture the match, fill tax-advantaged space, then taxable) and the right mix (pre-tax vs. Roth) sets up every decision in the rest of the book. The recurring ideas: tax-advantaged compounding, the free money of an employer match, tax diversification across buckets, and the inflation-protected foundation that Social Security and Treasury bonds provide.

PART II

Retirement Income

Decumulation

This is the harder, less-taught problem: turning a pile of savings into a paycheck that lasts the rest of your life. It's dominated by a system of interacting risks — inflation, healthcare, taxes, Medicare surcharges, market sequence, and longevity — and by the levers you finally control: which account to tap, when to claim Social Security, when to convert. These chapters cover safe withdrawal rates, the retiree-specific risks of inflation and healthcare, the guaranteed lifetime income you can add through pensions, annuities, and your home equity, and the tax levers you finally control: bracket-filling, IRMAA-aware withdrawals, Roth conversions, Social Security timing, sequencing, the required distributions that eventually force your hand, and the state that taxes it all.

Risk and the Long Run

Endurance

These chapters cover the risks that compound over decades, and the buffers, evidence, and hedges that keep a very long retirement intact.

PART V

Inheritance

Legacy

Whatever you don't spend passes to others — and how it's taxed depends entirely on the account type it sits in. With the federal estate tax now irrelevant for nearly every family, the real game is income-tax character and the step-up in basis. The guiding idea is simple: leave the step-up and tax-free assets to your heirs, and spend down or convert the pre-tax accounts yourself.
